# Before / After Examples

These examples show patterns, not templates. Never copy a name, gesture, object, or line of dialogue from an example into a user's draft. Apply the pattern to the actual content, and mark any detail you had to invent for author approval.

## Example 1: General Prose

**Before (AI-flavored)**:
In today's rapidly evolving digital landscape, it is important to note that leveraging robust tools can unlock a transformative experience. Moreover, these seamless solutions foster a rich tapestry of innovation that underscores the pivotal role of technology in empowering users.

**After (humanized)**:
Good tools make the work easier. The best ones disappear into the background so you can focus on what you're actually trying to do. Technology helps when it stays out of the way.

## Example 2: Academic-ish

**Before**:
This study delves into the intricate relationship between social media usage and adolescent mental health. It is crucial to understand that the findings underscore a comprehensive need for further research in this realm. Furthermore, the results highlight a transformative shift in how we approach digital well-being.

**After**:
We looked at how much time teenagers spend on social media and how that lines up with self-reported anxiety and sleep problems. The association is real but modest. More work is needed on whether the platforms themselves drive the changes or whether already-anxious kids simply use them more.

## Example 3: Narrative / Story

**Before**:
Her heart hammered as she embarked on a journey into the unknown. The tension was palpable, a rich tapestry of old fears and new hopes. It wasn't just fear; it was something deeper that underscored the pivotal moment.

**After**:
She stood at the edge of the platform longer than she meant to. The train was already there. She stepped on anyway, one hand still gripping the strap of her bag.

## Example 5: Defensive Academic (hedge stacking + reflexive limits)

**Before**:
Although this study may potentially offer some tentative insights, it is important to note that the findings should be interpreted with a great deal of caution. Some might argue that the observed association could possibly be explained by unmeasured confounding, and it should be acknowledged that our study is not without limitations. The cross-sectional design means that we cannot establish temporal order, and the sample was drawn from a single center, which may limit generalisability. It is worth noting that further research is needed before any firm conclusions can be drawn. The results may perhaps suggest that X could be associated with Y (OR 1.42, 95% CI 1.10–1.83; n = 1,204).

**After**:
The association between X and Y was modest (OR 1.42, 95% CI 1.10–1.83; n = 1,204). The sample came from a single center, which limits generalisability. Because the design is cross-sectional, we cannot establish temporal order, and unmeasured confounding remains possible. A longitudinal design with measured confounders would be needed to distinguish selection from effect.

**What changed**: thirteen defensive markers removed; effect size, confidence interval, sample size, single-center limit, and design limit kept; no causal upgrade; no new facts.

## Example 6: Defensive Narrative (reader management + authorial apology)

**Before**:
Of course, Mara was somewhat nervous as she walked into the room. Needless to say, she had every right to be. You might wonder why she didn't just leave; the answer is that she couldn't. She was a little bit afraid, but she tried her best to hide it. To be fair, anyone in her position would have felt the same way. It was, in some ways, a turning point in her life.

**After**:
Mara walked into the room. She stayed. She kept her hands still at her sides and her face flat, and when she spoke her voice was level.

**What changed**: reader management ("you might wonder"), authorial reassurance ("of course", "needless to say", "to be fair"), and abstract labels ("somewhat nervous", "a little bit afraid") removed; fear is rendered through posture and voice; no new names, dates, or plot events; the "turning point" is left implicit rather than announced. In real work, mark any concrete gesture the author did not supply for approval rather than treating it as fact.

## Example 7: Calibrated Caution Preserved (do not over-edit)

**Before**:
It is worth noting that because the analysis is cross-sectional, we cannot rule out reverse causation. The 95% CI for the primary estimate was wide (0.98–1.87), so the direction of the effect remains uncertain. Participants provided written informed consent, and the study was approved by the institutional review board.

**After**:
Because the analysis is cross-sectional, we cannot rule out reverse causation. The 95% CI for the primary estimate was wide (0.98–1.87), so the direction of the effect remains uncertain. Participants provided written informed consent, and the study was approved by the institutional review board.

**What changed**: one throat-clearing frame removed; the design limitation, the interval, the uncertainty, and the ethics disclosure kept. No new claims.
